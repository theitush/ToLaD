# Data sources (mammals v0)

**Policy (Ita, #429):** licences don't rule a source out; we credit every source and take down on request. The page's own list, one line per source, is [`web/src/fan/sources.ts`](../web/src/fan/sources.ts): a new source adds a line there.

All inputs are downloaded by `uv run tolad-fetch` into `data/raw/<source>/`, which is gitignored.
Each source gets its own directory. The downloads are only parsed by `pipeline/tolad_pipeline/`, never executed.
On the first download, `pipeline/sources.lock.json` records each file's URL, retrieval date, size and sha256. Later fetches verify against it, and a mismatch fails the fetch.

Rebuild everything from the raw downloads:

```sh
make -C pipeline          # fetch (verify checksums) + build + decoder round-trip test
# or step by step
cd pipeline && uv run tolad-fetch && uv run python -I -m tolad_pipeline.build && node --test decoder/test/roundtrip.test.mjs
```

Outputs go to `data/mammals/`: `skeleton.tld`, `t/<id>.tld`, `debug_top.json` and `stats.json` (match rates, fits, sizes, spot checks).
The format is described in [FORMAT.md](FORMAT.md).

### Considered, not used

- **Pacifici et al. 2013 on Dryad** (doi:10.5061/dryad.gd0m3): Dryad now refuses scripted downloads (HTTP 401/403). The same generation lengths come in through COMBINE.
- **AnAge** (HAGR): its license terms are unclear, and COMBINE + PanTHERIA already cover longevity and maturity.
- **OneZoom dated tree** (Duke et al. 2026): for all-life scale-out (plan step 5). Upham is the better mammal source.
  Its mammal ages are erratic against fossil-calibrated estimates (crown Canidae 32 Ma, Placentalia 129 Ma; `research/dated-tree-sources.md`).
- **TimeTree 5** (Kumar et al. 2022): closest to the fossil-calibrated consensus, but its terms forbid redistributing its data "and its transformations". Spot checks only.

## Manual overrides

Every hand-made change to what the sources say is in [`overrides.toml`](overrides.toml): versioned, one entry per change, each with a reason and a citation. The build fails if an entry stops matching anything, and lists what it applied in `stats.json` → `overrides`. Current entries:

- **Excluded:** `Tikitherium copei`. PBDB dates it to the Carnian (~230 Ma), but the single molar was re-described as a Neogene shrew: Averianov & Voyta 2024, *J. Mammal. Evol.* 31(1), doi:10.1007/s10914-024-09703-w.
- **Dates:** *Mammuthus subplanifrons* 6.2–3.75 Ma (PBDB's window midpoint gave 13.3 Ma; Sanders 2023), *M. meridionalis* from 2.6 Ma (Lister et al. 2005), *M. primigenius* from 0.6 Ma (Lister 2022), *M. lamarmorae* 0.3–0.04 Ma (Palombo et al. 2024). *Mammuthus* now starts at 6.4 Ma.
- **Hominins (ToLaD#275):** 23 first/last appearances from the literature in place of PBDB's wide windows (e.g. *Homo erectus* 2.0 Ma–108 ka, Rizal et al. 2020, *Nature* 577:381; Neanderthals 430–40 ka, Arsuaga et al. 2014 and Higham et al. 2014; *Sahelanthropus* 7.2–6.8 Ma, Lebatard et al. 2008; the rest as tabulated in Wood & Boyle 2016, *Am. J. Phys. Anthropol.* 159 S61:37–78, with the primary paper per entry). 22 `[[dated_fossil]]` entries hang them on the human lineage at their first appearance (grade-honest: their relationships are argued over), Neanderthals at their genomic split from us (550–765 ka, Prüfer et al. 2014, *Nature* 505:43). A [[date]] also stands in for occurrences PBDB lacks (*H. naledi*, Dirks et al. 2017; *Kenyanthropus platyops*, Leakey et al. 2001). Denisovans have no species record in PBDB or Open Tree here (Upham's `Homo_denisova` tip matches no leaf).
- **Placements:** Stagodontidae, Pediomyidae, *Kokopellia* and *Asiatherium* are Cretaceous metatherians outside crown Marsupialia (Williamson, Brusatte & Wilson 2014, *ZooKeys* 465:1–76). OTT and the PBDB graft put them under Didelphimorphia, which dragged the order back to 98 Ma. They now sit on the stem of Metatheria.

## PHYLACINE's map notes, classed by hand (ToLaD#507)

[`phylacine_notes.csv`](phylacine_notes.csv) has one row for each of the 1,069 PHYLACINE 1.2.1 species whose current and present-natural cells differ. Its source is PHYLACINE's own note on each change: Faurby et al. 2018, *Ecology* 99:2626, `Data/Ranges/Spatial_metadata.csv` in the range-map zip above, columns `Modification`, `Motivation`, `Certainty.Level`. The 400 distinct note texts were read one by one, and each species got a class from what its note says:

- `people`: the change is people's doing by the note's own reasoning, or the note sits in PHYLACINE's human-impact codes 1–5 (historic range, introductions, disjunct ranges, whole islands, suitable areas next to a known decline) and says nothing else;
- `taxonomy`: range split, reassigned or lumped between species;
- `mapping`: records deleted as errors, misidentifications or rare migrants;
- `placeholder`: no real range is known, so the note sets one (a town near the type locality, a borrowed range, fossil-site estimates for extinct species);
- `unclear`: none so far.

Columns: `binomial`, `iucn` (PHYLACINE's status), `cells_cur`, `cells_nat`, `class`, `why` (ours), `phylacine_note` (the note's own key phrase, verbatim), `partner` (the other species the note moves land to or from, as the note names it; empty for none).

**Today's map (ToLaD#507).** For a living species classed `taxonomy` or `mapping`, today's count (density × range × occupancy) stands on the map the note corrects, worked out cell by cell from the rasters (`ranges.today_maps`): its present-natural map where that is its current map with land moved to or from its `partner` (every lost cell in the partner's present-natural map, every gained cell in the partner's current map) or with the deleted records taken off; for the partner, its current map minus the cells the note gives away. A note that also adds PHYLACINE's own expansion or historic range, or that names no mapped partner, leaves the species as it is. `stats.json` → `phylacine_ranges.today_maps` lists each one, with the reason. A species whose area comes from PanTHERIA, or whose count is a census, keeps it.

For an extant species classed `taxonomy`, `mapping` or `placeholder`, the build (`model.natural_level`) takes today's count as the natural level. No range-loss trend comes from the map difference, and no level is fitted to a placeholder area. Extinct species are not changed: their present-natural maps are what their counts stand on. `stats.json` → `natural_baseline.phylacine_note_today` lists the leaves this applies to. A test checks that the file still matches PHYLACINE's metadata row for row.

## Stem fossils (automatic)

Taxonomy often files a fossil inside a crown group that it predates. The dating rule "a parent is at least as old as its oldest child" then drags the crown node and every extant lineage under it back to the fossil's age. Examples are Cretaceous "didelphimorphs", Paleocene "carnivorans", Eocene equids and the Cretaceous "soricid" *Cretasorex*. These old crowns then soaked up deep-time ghost lineages.

Now a fossil-only subtree older than the upper end of the Upham 95% HPD of an enclosing crown node counts as a stem member. It moves next to that crown, inside a new node that carries the taxon's name (the total group). The crown keeps its molecular age and is renamed "*name* (crown)". This is a rule, not an override. The 2026-10-09 build moves 782 fossil subtrees into 115 total groups, listed in `stats.json` → `stem_rehoming`. Crown nodes not calibrated by Upham can still be raised by fossils.

## Counts calibration

Wild standing populations come from, in order of preference:
1. a global population report from an IUCN assessment, as compiled by Greenspoon et al. 2023 (479 species)
2. Greenspoon's per-species model estimate (≈4,350 land species)
3. density × range × occupancy *k*. *k* is fitted per order group (the nine orders Greenspoon report, plus "Other") as a function of body mass, on the species that have 1 or 2. Marine species use a mass + order fit on the marine census species.

Dogs (1e9) and cats (6e8) come from Greenspoon's pets table and get alive grade C. FAOSTAT livestock stays at grade B. Both are added on top of the wild lineage. Domestic forms with no wild population of their own (cattle, sheep, horse, donkey, dromedary, water buffalo, cat) keep their modelled wild ancestor only up to domestication.

Wild land individuals come to 8.4e10 (Greenspoon: 8.8e10). Land biomass is 34 Mt, inside Greenspoon's 13–38 Mt CI but above their 20 Mt median, because census species use PHYLACINE adult masses. Details are in `stats.json` → `occupancy_calibration`.

## Ghost lineages

The ghost share of Mammalia's ever-lived count is about 95%. The computation is sound: an extant species' band only reaches back to its split from its sister, a few Myr, and PBDB samples only a small fraction of fossil species. By era, the share is about 7% in the last 2.6 Myr, 77% in the Neogene, 99% in the Paleogene and 100% before 66 Ma (`stats.json` → `ghost_lineages.by_era`). Ghosts are kept separate in `GHST`.

They are allocated by crown membership. At time t, the deficit is split over the reconstructed lineages crossing t, weighted by the present-day wild births of each lineage's extant descendants. It is credited to the clade in whose crown that lineage runs. So:
- a crown order collects ghosts only after its own crown split
- deep-time ghosts go to Mammalia, Theria and Placentalia-level nodes
- relict lineages and stem fossils do not soak them up

The old allocation, by lineage rate over any covering band, let old-looking orders such as Didelphimorphia, Macroscelidea and Monotremata outrank Rodentia.

Mammalia's ever-lived total is 8.5e18 (95% range 1e17–1e21). That is just under 02's 1e19 lower bound. 02 assumed about 3e11 wild mammals alive; the Greenspoon-calibrated count is 8.5e10, and T_eff × wild births scales with it.

## Known quirks

- PBDB API responses start with a volatile `"elapsed_time"` field. The fetcher rewrites it to 0 before hashing, so the pinned checksum covers the records only. The lock was re-pinned on 2026-10-09: same records, byte length −4.
- PBDB species with no occurrences or no first/last appearance are skipped. So are names that are not plain binomials (`cf.`, subgenera in the name, and so on).
- Infraspecific Open Tree tips are folded into their species, e.g. *Canis lupus familiaris* → *Canis lupus*. Tips that are not species are dropped.
- Where the synthesis tree does not resolve a family as a node (a "broken" taxon such as Hominidae), the family is placed at the MRCA of the species Upham assigns to it, if that MRCA is tight.
- A living Open Tree tip that no extant-species list recognises (Upham, PHYLACINE, the IUCN Red List 2026-1 by name, synonym or spelling variant, Greenspoon's census and model tables, the domestic tables) is dropped (ToLaD#116): such tips are subspecies (*Felis affinis*), stale spellings (*Puma yaguarondi*), synonyms (*Lynx vulgaris*) and unflagged fossils, whose individuals the recognised species already counts. Where a recognised leaf knows the name as a synonym, the name is kept as that leaf's alt name. A fossil PBDB knows comes back as a graft. See `stats.json` → `unrecognised_tips`.
- A leaf the Red List 2026-1 assesses EX is extinct even where PHYLACINE 1.2 (Red List 2016-3) still has it living (ToLaD#105). No extinct leaf reaches the present: one that is EX in the Red List or PHYLACINE (ended after 1500 CE) ends no later than 100 years ago, any other no later than 1500 CE, the start of the Red List's EX window, however young its last PBDB record.
- The domestic forms whose wild ancestor is another leaf (llama, alpaca, zebu, domestic yak, Bactrian camel) have no wild population today (ToLaD#135): their wild lineage runs to domestication only and, as FAOSTAT has no item for any of them, they carry no domestic stock (`anchors.DOMESTIC_FORM_NO_STOCK`).
- The living species described or split since Upham's taxonomy (~2017) come from MDD v2.5 (ToLaD#278, `mdd.py`). An Open Tree tip MDD accepts as a living species is recognised (not dropped by #116). Each living MDD species is matched to our living leaves by its name, then by its own synonyms (its nominal names in its genus, original combination, MSW3/CMW names, the name the Red List assessed it under), then by our OTT/PHYLACINE synonyms; a leaf is claimed once, and never through another MDD species' accepted name, so a split's daughter does not take the parent's leaf. Each living wild MDD species left over is grafted on its genus: under the common ancestor of the leaves its MDD genus-mates matched; with one genus-mate, under a new split above that leaf (dated by interpolation); with none, in a new genus node under its family-mates (else order-mates). It carries its MDD English name (when the Red List has none) and its Red List status from the 2026-1 list. Its count: a census or Greenspoon estimate by name if there is one; else the occupancy model if it has a range of its own (PanTHERIA or PHYLACINE); else the median today's count of the other species of its MDD genus (else family, else order), grade D, error the taxon's spread (min 0.5 dex); for a species MDD lists CR or EW, at most 250 (IUCN CR criterion C), error 1 dex; listed in `stats.json` → `mdd.genus_median_estimates`. An Open Tree tip only MDD knows by name is counted the same way. A synonym alone does not keep a tip (one MDD species has many). Our living leaves MDD does not recognise are listed (not changed) in `stats.json` → `mdd.ours_not_in_mdd`.
